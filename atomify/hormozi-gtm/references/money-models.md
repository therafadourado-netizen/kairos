## 1. Core thesis
A **Money Model** is a *deliberate sequence of offers* — what you offer, when, and how — engineered to make as much money from a customer as fast as possible. The point is **Client-Financed Acquisition**: enough profit in the first 30 days that one customer funds acquiring + servicing at least two more, so cash stops being the growth bottleneck. Baseline good = more profit than cost to get+service in 30 days; a **$100M Money Model** = one customer's 30-day profit exceeds the cost to get+service *many*.

## 2. Frameworks

### Client-Financed Acquisition
Make more money from customers than it costs to get them — fast. **30-Day Rule:** cover CAC within 30 days (mirrors an interest-free credit cycle; clear before month end, recycle the cash). Either wait years to recoup and pray, or get paid fast and grow freely — a good model is the latter. Compounding: 2× value × 2× volume × 2× speed = **8×** (3× each = 27×).

### The Four Types of Offers
- **Attraction** — turn strangers into customers (free/discount).
- **Upsell** — get people to spend more (whatever you offer next).
- **Downsell** — turn no into yes (whatever you offer after a no).
- **Continuity** — keep people buying (ongoing value for ongoing payments).
The most profitable businesses use all four.

### Attraction Offers (5) — free/discount/$1 are a continuum
1. **Win Your Money Back** — do X in Y within Z rules → free. Qualify by Results / Actions / both. Criteria must be Easy to Track, Get Results, Advertise the business. Real profit from those who succeed AND have something else to buy → **have an upsell ready**; store credit = cash (apply as per-month discount, don't give free months). Only if refund rate <5%.
2. **Giveaways** — enter to win a Grand Prize (= the thing you want everyone to buy; value-anchor it). One winner; everyone else gets a discounted promotional offer. 6 steps; discount = **10–30% of gross margin**; urgency in 3 places (enter/claim/use); downsells ready. If it doesn't work, the Grand Prize wasn't grand enough. Heavily regulated — check sweepstakes law.
3. **Decoy** — advertise a stripped/cheap version; present it beside a premium (more features/bonuses/guarantee). Sell benefits not features; make contrast huge; present premium first; ask whether they came for free stuff or lasting results. Discount offers have higher show-up than free (use where no-show is costly). 4 ways to state a discount (%, $, free portion, total package) — test.
4. **Buy X Get Y Free** — reframe a discount as free product by selling multiples. Raise prices before giving away; give more free than paid; more cheap free things can beat fewer expensive; lengthens duration. Manage prepaid cash. Cap ~10% of customers for a cash pop.
5. **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** — choice: full price later (delayed payment + conditional guarantee + card on file) or discounted now (20–50% off + bonuses). Removes risk; lets you advertise free + capture card. If >10% of pay-later cancel → offer too weak.

### Upsell Offers (4) — often most of the profit
Offer More / Better / New. Fail when wrong thing / wrong time / wrong way.
1. **Classic** — solve the next problem the moment it appears; offer higher-profit upsells first; say-no-to-say-yes; surprise & delight; exploit the Hyper Buying Cycle; BAMFAM; the 3-option price nudge.
2. **Menu** — Unselling (what they DON'T need) + Prescription (what they DO need + how) + A/B (which, not whether) + Card-on-File.
3. **Anchor** — present a premium (5–10×) first → the gasp → rescue with the main offer (now looks cheap). Some still buy the premium. Always have a premium offer ready for big budgets.
4. **Rollover** — credit a past purchase toward a bigger next offer (≥4× the credit → ≤25% discount). Who: lapsed/upset/competitors'/current. Winback campaigns; one-time urgency; gift-card play (~20% of next offer's price).

### Downsell Offers (3) — after a no. Never the same thing cheaper.
Change *how they pay* or *what they get*. Downsells are trades; personalize don't pressure.
1. **Payment Plan** — same product/price spread over time. 7-step ladder: reward-for-paying-in-full → 3rd-party financing/card/layaway → half now/half later → want-check (1–10, need 8+) → ⅓ down + 2 → even spread → free trial. Align to paychecks; re-run declined cards; keep the same % paying in full. Churn by cadence: monthly 10.7% / quarterly 5% / annual 2% → start high.
2. **Trial With Penalty** — meet the terms → start free; pay only if you don't. Mirror Win-Your-Money-Back criteria. Get the card first, then explain fees. Upsell by scenario (like it / hate it / didn't use it).
3. **Feature Downsell** — lower price by removing features (highest-value first → they re-choose the original; lowest-value + big drop → they take the downsell). Name combos (aspirational top; cheapest = "The Minimum"). Barter with reviews/testimonials/referrals.

### Continuity Offers (3) — placed LAST; combine bonuses/discounts/fees
1. **Continuity Bonus** — bonus if they sign up today (bonus value > first payment); advertise the bonus, not the membership. Standalone-vs-continuity price ratio steers the mix: 1.33×→50%, 1.66×→60%, 2×→70%, 2.33×→80%, 2.66×→90% choose continuity. Bulk prepaid upsell raises 30-day profit sharply even at 1-in-8 take.
2. **Continuity Discount** — free time for committing. Apply up front / at end / spread / after 1–2 payments. **Bill in 4-week cycles** (13/yr vs 12 = +8.3% revenue). Add 3% processing fee; capture a 2nd payment method (ACH).
3. **Waived Fee** — setup fee (3–5× monthly) waived for a term commitment; pay it if you cancel early. Customers stay longer when leaving costs more than staying. If >5% cancel early, investigate.

### Sequencing — three stages
Stage I Get Cash (Attraction) → Stage II Get More Cash (Upsell & Downsell) → Stage III Get the Most Cash (Continuity). Evolution: get customers reliably → make them pay for themselves → make them pay for other customers → maximize LTV → spend max on ads. Each stage funds the next. Never deploy the whole model at once.

## 3. Step-by-step (design a money model)
Start with an Attraction Offer (cover CAC+delivery) → add an Upsell at the next problem / point of greatest need → add a Downsell for the nos (change how-pay or what-get) → add Continuity last (then bulk-prepaid upsell rolling into recurring) → perfect one offer at a time → raise prices in stages until profit peaks → fill gaps with affiliate products → keep it simple (Simple Scales, Fancy Fails).

## 4. Decision rules
Attraction→Upsell→Downsell→Continuity (continuity last); build stage-by-stage · 30-day cash > CAC; ideal 1 funds 2+ · higher-profit upsells first; anchor 5–10× first · after a no never same-thing-cheaper · 1–10 want-check, proceed at 8+ · rollover next offer ≥4× credit · giveaway discount 10–30% of margin · WYMB refunds <5%, Pay-Later cancels ≤10%, Waived-Fee early cancels ≤5% · 4-week billing, align to paychecks, +3% fee, capture ACH · standalone:continuity ratio 1.33–2.66× for 50–90% continuity · start prices high then work down · BAMFAM · give refunds when asked — hard selling is for weak products.

## 5. Review checklist (money-model audit)
Does one customer's 30-day profit cover their CAC+delivery? Fund 2+ more? Self-financing in a 30-day cycle? · Distinct Attraction Offer with enough up-front cash (multiple times over)? Discount tested vs free where no-show costly? Giveaway prize grand + anchored + urgency? · Defined upsell at greatest need, higher-profit first, anchor available, BAMFAM? · Defined downsell that changes how-pay/what-get (never same cheaper), no cannibalization of full-pays, 1–10 check used? · Continuity last, 4-week billing, processing fee, backup/ACH captured, churn managed (cadence, lifetime discount at churn point, easy cancel path)? · One offer perfected at a time? Prices raised in stages? Refund/cancel thresholds respected? Simple (many offers, one product)? Legal/transparent + refunds honored?

## 6. Common mistakes
Bad models kill businesses (spend more to get a customer than they yield) · only a front end (no upsell) · waiting years to recoup CAC · discounting the same product to close · dropping price out of fear · giving free stuff without raising prices / mismanaging prepaid cash · treating the anchor as fake · unbelievable bonus values · continuity as a standalone attraction offer · deploying a full model at once · explaining trial fees before getting the card · hard-to-cancel (silent churn + 1-stars) · copying competitors' models · upsell wrong thing/time/way · the WYMB myth (real money is from people who succeed and buy more, not from failures).
